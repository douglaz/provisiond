**Q16a verdict — the A→B counterexample is possible under the unverified provider premise; do not enable the identity shortcut yet.** It is a hypothetical trace, not an observed Robot failure. [PRV-42](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/02-provider-contract.md#L1084) says equality “by rule rather than by coincidence” is `[verify]` *before a driver depends on it*. If V8’s positive-only guard replaces that precondition, its negative branch can release the commitment after `GET /server/A` misses while B exists: [PRV-42’s table](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/02-provider-contract.md#L1064), [OPS-27’s release row](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/03-operation-lifecycle.md#L457), [V8](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/docs/research/provider-verify-2026-10.md#L233).

The smaller rule is to use the auction channel’s existing fingerprint search, then directly read the **server number returned by the matched transaction**. [PRV-32](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/02-provider-contract.md#L758) already requires a unique per-order key; [OPS-27](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/03-operation-lifecycle.md#L464) already requires the confirming resource read. A positive read of A alone also cannot attribute A to this order: [PRV-42’s claim](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/02-provider-contract.md#L1054) excludes other provisiond creates in flight, not purchases outside provisiond. Once a fingerprint-matched transaction supplies the server number, comparing it with the offer adds no resolution evidence.

This costs listing requests and ordering access, and automatic search ends at the [documented 30-day listing horizon](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/docs/research/provider-verify-2026-10.md#L217). Your “unavailable evidence leaves the order unresolved” needs one qualification: [OPS-33](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/03-operation-lifecycle.md#L1432) releases a commitment after its bounded **negative** window while the operation stays open, but explicitly does **not** run that window against a matched order still in progress. Keep offer-number identity `[verify]`; two positive live examples cannot establish the universal negative-read rule.

**Q16b verdict — share one consumption guard across driver cleanup and the sweep; operation status cannot be that guard.** The Manager’s conflict is exact: [PRV-9](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/02-provider-contract.md#L131) says keys “MUST NOT” be removed until consumed, while [OPS-32](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/03-operation-lifecycle.md#L1421) says a tagged key whose operation is not `running` “is **deleted**.” A crash or ambiguous accepted create can reach `needs_reconciliation` before an asynchronous provider finishes with its key.

Your proposed milestones are conservative cleanup points from [V2](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/docs/research/provider-verify-2026-10.md#L117), [V18](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/docs/research/provider-verify-2026-10.md#L448), and [V19](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/docs/research/provider-verify-2026-10.md#L466), not proof that every “machine is up”: Cherry’s iPXE case uses `allocated`, and rescue needs the boot that consumes its key. Keep the precise consumption timing `[verify]`. Delete after an observed safe milestone or authoritative evidence that no consumer remains; retain and surface unknown cases. A definitively refused create or terminal cancelled provider order may supply that latter evidence. An operation has no `cancelled` status, and [OPS-3](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/03-operation-lifecycle.md#L79) says `failed` establishes “nothing about its side effects.” Thus “delete on settlement, whatever the outcome” is unsafe. Nor does later settlement alone prove a lingering key safe to delete. Robot additionally needs [V9’s still-unverified fingerprint survival](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/843a2f28650922383fc355972720cd69ac743fa0/docs/research/provider-verify-2026-10.md#L249) before key deletion can leave its search intact.

I found no documented **per-key fee or stored-key count cap** in the consulted provider sources; that is not a guarantee of unlimited keys. Published request limits do affect cleanup: [Robot’s key API](https://robot.hetzner.com/doc/webservice/en.html) limits key mutations, and [DigitalOcean](https://docs.digitalocean.com/reference/api/reference/public-apis/) limits key-list reads. The strongest smaller design is a provider-evidence guard plus operator-visible retained keys, without a generic status-based cleanup state machine.
