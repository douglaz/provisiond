## 1. Objection

The objection to Option 2 is its own sentence: "An engine that acknowledged no write after the backup: its memory is the restored store's, and it runs until `STO-54`'s record commits a fresh generation." I concede it, and I can bound it but not close it.

**The bound.** The chain is only idle while the engine process writes nothing, and the spec makes that short for any deployment with a fleet. `LDG-37` (12:523): "the meter writes at least one transaction per billable subject per interval", a row on `OVR-19` (00:414, "Metering cadence"), and `OVR-17` (00:316) puts "the three `ledger` rows" in the engine process. `OPS-32` (03:1550-1551): "A complete pass MUST write the observed state and `machines.state_observed_at` (`STO-48`) for every managed machine it observed present." So with one managed machine the survivor has an acknowledged post-backup write within one Metering cadence and one Account-sweep interval, and the chain refuses it. `STO-54`'s own danger list needs such a machine: "an extension lost in Δ routes a paid machine into `LDG-14`" (05:1489). In shape 1, Form R narrows the residue further: after the lock loss the engine "claim[s] nothing more and begin[s] no periodic pass", so its only writes are stop records, guarded on `(id, status = running, claim_number = mine)` (`STO-3`). A row claimed after the backup is `queued` in the rewound store, a zero-row write under `OPS-22`; a row claimed before it holds the engine's own claim number, and the record is the engine's own provider outcome.

**What survives the bound.** A restore whose Δ is shorter than one metering interval. That is not exotic: a point-in-time recovery to minutes before an operator error is the common restore. In shape 2 the engine sees no lock loss, keeps claiming and running periodic passes against the rewound server, and Option 4's trace holds as written: "Even if that transaction increments a store-wide counter, E has not observed the new counter." The `api` process is unobserved by the chain in every shape. So in the misconfigured case the owner asked about, Option 2 answers "the survivor writes until the record lands, forever if it is skipped" for exactly the window the check exists to close. Conceded.

Two further defects in Option 2's text, recorded though I do not vote for it: its `ADR-0047` line "no model carries a restore, so no theorem" is false (see Brief errors); and its `CNF-370` case (b) asserts that "every write the survivor commits before the record carries a number above the backup's highest", which fails an implementation that is safer than the chain and refuses those writes.

## 2. Vote

**Option 1 as written, S2 included.** No hybrid.

- **It covers every restore `STO-54` specifies.** The posture is physical: "continuous WAL archiving" (05:1495-1496) and promotion. postgresql.org 18's recovery procedure, checked today, has "Stop the server, if it's running." as step 1 and "Start the server." as step 9, and no other stop or start. Every specified restore starts a server, so the start instant differs for every survivor of one. Shape 3 is off-procedure and gets Reader Z's sentence, scoped.
- **It passes the owner's test where it matters.** Store opened early, record skipped: the survivor is refused in shapes 1 and 2 by a value no restore can bring back, because its writer is the server.
- **Only it checks `api`.** `STO-54` lists "a token revoked in Δ is live again (`API-56`)". In shape 2 a surviving `api` replica serves the rewound store, and Base, Option 2, Option 3 and Option 4 check none of its writes. S2 exits it on the first.
- **Complexity.** One server value in the first statement G already makes. Option 2's table grows one row per engine write, which `LDG-37` makes at least one per billable subject per metering interval, deleted in the advance transaction at every startup, and the Appendix's timeouts cover "Its replacing statement" only, not that delete.
- **The cost, accepted and not re-argued.** After any PostgreSQL stop and start, R's records are refused and `OPS-15` classifies the rows. Against pv-buc's example that is a regression whenever the lock loss was a PostgreSQL restart. It is degraded, not unsafe: `OPS-27` resolves the create by correlator and posts its money in its terminal transaction. A later bead could test whether `OPS-21`'s records after a detected lock loss, already guarded by status and claim number, can be exempted from the instant comparison. Not proposed here.

## 3. ACCEPT

**ACCEPT** Option 1 on top of the Base and the Appendix, read whole. I find no `CNF` assertion or Lean theorem it flips, makes unreachable or vacuous, no MUST with a breaking trace, and no trace in which it admits a stale write. The refused writes after a plain restart are refused by design and stated in its own `OPS-50` (b). `CNF-366` terminates a backend and starts no server, so its records land. `CNF-362` starts no server. `superseded_never_commits` and `generationCheck` (F4) model a mismatch, which a pair comparison still is.

Non-blocking notes, with replacement text:

- **S2's `api` instant has no referent on a normal start.** "the instant the process read with `STO-56`'s open row": `STO-56` has "at most one row may have `closed_at` null", so on most starts no row is open. Replacement: "the instant the process read at startup in the statement that reads `STO-56`'s open row, whether or not a row is open". And in `STO-56`: "; `api` reads `pg_postmaster_start_time()` in that statement whether or not a row is open (`OPS-50`)".
- **`CNF-370` (i) and (ii): the loss is the stop.** R says "treat the loss as a stop of every worker not already recording one", so in (i) and (ii) the held worker is cancelled before "then release the worker". Replacement for that clause: "then release the worker, or let the stop the lock loss delivers cancel it (`OPS-47`); the write under test is its settle or cut-off record".
- **`ADR-0047`'s restart-cost paragraph should quote R's sentence it narrows**, per AGENTS.md: `OPS-47` (Appendix 2) "record each under `OPS-21` within its Stop-record bound, subject to its exits", with the superseded exit named as the exit that applies after a server start.

## Brief errors

- **Option 2's text, reproduced unflagged:** "no model carries a restore, so no theorem." `tools/formal/Provisiond/Restore.lean`:3-6: "A live engine commits a `History` of steps on a `Store` and crashes; what it comes up on is either that store (`RestartTrace`) or a store `Δ` steps behind it with the provider's facts unchanged (`RestoreTrace`)", and `Witnesses.lean`:1191 holds `restore_breaks_soundness`.
