## 1. Verdicts

**C-WITNESS: CANNOT ACCEPT as written.** Decisive reason: its clean certificate requires a fact no evidence can supply and the form forbids the only substitute. It says the procedure "MUST cover every such path, including privileged administration", that an invariant counts "only if its coverage and non-bypass throughout that history are themselves established", and that "A missing event record, without complete coverage, MUST NOT be interpreted as evidence that no event occurred." A PostgreSQL superuser's `pg_terminate_backend` and root's signals cannot be intercepted by any evidence service; the audit already says "There is no adopted storage protocol, trusted issuer or proof that these records cannot be lost, bypassed or forged" (lines 694–696). Read strictly, no deployment can ever issue the certificate and the restart path is reachable only in fixtures that construct coverage by test oracle. Read leniently, "non-bypass" is the named issuer's trusted attestation, which the form never labels as trust the way `STO-7` labels its configuration duty ("Provisiond relies on that duty"). The form must say which; as printed it is either unsatisfiable or an unlabelled trust posture bought with mandatory infrastructure.

**C-UNKNOWN: ACCEPT as written.** It stays inside every fixed boundary, and where its negative-evidence standard bottoms out in the same unattainable "established coverage and non-bypass", it supplies an explicit conforming fallback: "A deployment unable to supply the negative evidence may use the operator boundary below." That makes the form satisfiable by every deployment, at the cost of automatic failover. Open terms and costs are in parts 4 and 6; they are owner matters, not defects of the predicate.

**NEITHER: ACCEPT as written.** Byte-identical retention; nothing checked or normative moves. The audit itself records that the policy is complete and only knowability is open: "Proper classification is already a restore, with the existing operator decision and procedure, not a new rule proposed here" (lines 367–369).

**Final: NEITHER.** Preference is separate from acceptance: I can accept C-UNKNOWN; I prefer not to send it yet because both amendment candidates build refusal or evidence machinery on a historical condition whose breadth the owner has not yet decided (part 6, decision 1). ## 3. C-WITNESS ordering and completeness

- **Abrupt primary, host or power loss.** What survives is the active-incarnation record and any acknowledged intents. The form correctly refuses absence inference, so the interval to the crash is "unresolved" unless "independent evidence excludes" a termination. The only such evidence is the claim that every controlled path was intercepted. That claim is the issuer's attestation, not an observation.
- **Evidence service failure.** "A failed or unacknowledged intent write MUST prevent that controlled signal." While the service is down the primary cannot be shut down in an orderly or fast way; the only exits are uncontrolled, which taint the interval. Separately, "Before a primary incarnation first serves component work, the deployment MUST establish an independently durable record", so a promoted or restored primary cannot serve components until the service answers. The form claims unknown endpoints do not bar a restore "once its existing startup prerequisites are met", but it has just added a new one. This is the audit's "indefinite ambiguity if that path failed", now also an availability coupling.
- **Privileged bypass.** Superuser SQL and root signals produce no intent. A certificate asserting complete coverage is then false, and nothing in the form detects it. The audit's own sentence applies: "a dishonest/misconfigured evidence source could falsely say 'known clean'."
- **Wrong ancestry.** Records must be "bound to ... the actual WAL ancestry", while "Matching timeline labels or larger numerical positions alone do not establish that binding." System identifier, timeline id and LSN are the only identities an intent writer can know before signalling. The form demands a binding it does not define and rules out the only available one. This is a second impossible certificate, shared by C-UNKNOWN's "evidence valid for that database, incarnation and actual WAL ancestry".
- **Unclosed intent.** Stays conservative: resolvable "only with authenticated, continuously covered evidence". A drain-first shutdown (components stopped, no `SyncRep` waiter observed, then signal) can carry that evidence in the intent itself. That is the one path where the contract is clean and cheap.

Can a clean certificate be soundly issued? Not from evidence. It can be issued as a trusted attestation of non-bypass by a deployment-named issuer. So the form supplies a real contract for controlled signals, active registration and crash intervals, and hides the original problem inside "the deployment MUST name the trusted issuer". Uncertainty must remain over every interval the issuer cannot show it owned, which in a real deployment is every interval.

## 4. C-UNKNOWN predicate; NEITHER's remaining obligation

**Positive case.** Yes, a positive case exists: authenticated off-primary records with claimed complete coverage, or an "independently enforced no-event invariant". In deployment that is again an attestation; the check's fixture 1 accepts it, and fixture 2's "Do not give the procedure the test's hidden oracle" is the real new test, which a belief-driven flag fails. So the positive control is reachable, but only by a deployment that built C-WITNESS-grade records. The form is narrower in text and equal in practice for anyone who wants automatic failover.

**Asymmetry worth the owner's attention.** The first paragraph lets any condition be established "by a trusted deployment invariant whose uninterrupted coverage establishes the fact", which is how the "committed without one of them" condition survives without a drift controller. The second paragraph refuses the same trust for the termination condition: it demands an "independently enforced" invariant with "non-bypass". Nothing in the form justifies trusting `STO-7`'s configuration duty while distrusting `STO-7`'s termination prohibition. If the prohibition were trusted equally, the unknown fact would shrink to one question: was the primary ever shut down smart or fast while a backend waited. That is a far cheaper fact to record.

**Drift controller:** not required; the form says so and the trusted-invariant clause carries it. **Reset:** none; "do not expire that condition". **Endpoint bar:** none; explicit. **Undefined terms:** "incarnation" (absent from `CONTEXT.md`), "authenticated source", and the ancestry binding above.

**NEITHER's precise obligation.** The deployment must not classify as a restart any promotion after the primary "had a waiting backend cancelled or terminated", shutdown included, with no expiry. From identical survivors the paired traces cannot both be handled: same input, different required class. A conforming deployment therefore needs one of three things outside the survivors: retained knowledge (what C-WITNESS specifies), prevention (drain before every shutdown, and trust that no individual termination occurs, which `STO-7` already forbids), or refusal. Refusal avoids the violation, since "otherwise leaves the store without a primary until both standbys report or an operator decides", but leaves recovery to an operator for whom the current words set no evidence standard. Note the difference from C-UNKNOWN that the brief warns about: under NEITHER an automatic restart on an actually clean history is conforming even when the promoter could not know; under C-UNKNOWN it is a violation.
