**Verdict: Reopen Q23a’s threat model, add an account-level server-read exhaustion alarm, and do not pre-fund the read-back tail.**

The [live session](docs/research/panels-2026-10/r45/live-session2.txt:19) proves that reads of two different servers, the account list, and cancellation data were refused together. It used one account, webservice user, and source IP, so it cannot separate those scopes. The cancel POST succeeded while reads were blocked. The probes show that refused reads did not prolong *this* block; they do not establish a one-hour maximum for future outages.

A tenant with one machine cannot spend 200 **successful** refresh reads quickly while the sweep keeps its observation fresh: [DOM-8](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/935b316a600515c118e027fee830e6e7136ec74a/01-domain-model.md#L143) answers those refreshes from cache. Once reads fail, retries can repeat, but the live probes did not keep the block alive. [OPS-27](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/935b316a600515c118e027fee830e6e7136ec74a/03-operation-lifecycle.md#L474) adds direct confirmation reads for creates; [OPS-32](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/935b316a600515c118e027fee830e6e7136ec74a/03-operation-lifecycle.md#L1403) adds a read for each missing-machine candidate. The claimed 20/day cap applies to **each order endpoint**, not deletes ([provider notes](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/935b316a600515c118e027fee830e6e7136ec74a/08-provider-notes.md#L444), [Robot documentation](https://robot.hetzner.com/doc/webservice/en.html)). Deletes have a configurable *per-principal* ceiling ([SEC-39](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/935b316a600515c118e027fee830e6e7136ec74a/07-security-requirements.md#L212)); enough paid machines and an accommodating ceiling can therefore spend the shared reads. The specification does not prove that tenant traffic cannot exhaust them.
