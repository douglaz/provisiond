**Verdict: other — gate offers by egress liability.** Sell the Hetzner Robot offers with the documented unlimited-traffic 1 Gbit uplink; withhold metered-egress Cloud and DigitalOcean offers from sale until a provider-enforced cap or a separately approved funding design exists. This changes the launch decision in [ADR-0010](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/docs/adr/0010-v1-ships-three-drivers-across-two-companies.md#L28). It is a substantial product tradeoff, but a new byte meter alone cannot bound a prepaid operator’s loss: usage can outrun a fixed commitment before a trustworthy reading arrives. [PRV-13b](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/02-provider-contract.md#L162) requires the reseller to “bound its own exposure before taking money.”

- **Speed and arithmetic.** The [Robot traffic page](https://docs.hetzner.com/robot/general/traffic/) assigns the dedicated 1 Gbit uplink to *root servers*. Hetzner’s [Cloud technical FAQ](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/technical-details/faq/) describes a shared 10 Gbit host link, gives no Cloud bandwidth guarantee, and says to expect about 300–500 Mbit/s. Thus 1 Gbit/s on `cpx11` is unverified, not an assured rate. Using your reported 20 TiB API allowance and $1.20/TB, sustained 300–500 Mbit/s exhausts it in **6.8–4.1 days**, then costs **$3.89–$6.48/day**; 30 days yields **$90–$168** in overage. Your 1 Gbit/s calculation is arithmetically sound as a scenario ($12.96/day after 2.04 days), but its Cloud speed premise is unsupported. No attacker is required; an ordinary sustained outbound workload incurs the same bill. Hetzner’s [2026 price adjustment](https://docs.hetzner.com/general/infrastructure-and-availability/price-adjustment/) lists `CPX11` at $0.0328/hour, or $0.787/day **before** customer margin. Your ~$0.28/day matches its older $0.0112/hour provider price, not a verified current customer rate.

- **Allowance and period.** Hetzner’s [Cloud billing FAQ](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/billing/faq/) describes “your server” with included traffic and confirms outgoing-only billing, 100 MB overage blocks, and alerts that do not stop charges. The [API changelog](https://docs.hetzner.cloud/changelog) places allowance and overage price by tariff and location. Those public texts do **not** establish whether a short-lived server’s allowance is prorated, expressly rule out project pooling, or promise when its traffic counter updates; those points need provider confirmation or invoice evidence. The Robot page says calendar months, but does not establish the UTC boundary required by [LDG-68](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/12-billing-and-ledger.md#L57). DigitalOcean is different: [Droplet bandwidth](https://docs.digitalocean.com/platform/billing/bandwidth/) accrues with lifetime, pools **allowance and usage across the team**, charges $0.01/GiB beyond the pool, and shows billing-page projections updated daily. A per-Droplet threshold cannot identify which tenant owes a pooled overage without a new allocation rule.

- **Why (a) fails as proposed.** [DOM-8](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/01-domain-model.md#L125) calls *machine state* a cache; its account sweep is not a specified byte meter. The provider API’s “current billing period” counter has no documented freshness guarantee or final reading after deletion. [LDG-38](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/12-billing-and-ledger.md#L588) prices elapsed-time increments with a time high-water mark; [ADR-0033](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/docs/adr/0033-a-machines-first-billing-unit-is-prepaid-machine-time.md#L19) prepays the first **time** unit. Neither settles cumulative bytes or month-end 100 MB rounding. A separate byte mark, provider-period identity, correction rule, and likely a `traffic_debit` entry kind are needed; [LDG-7](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/12-billing-and-ledger.md#L112) currently has no such kind. At-cost still creates a second customer-facing price dimension; [ADR-0006](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/docs/adr/0006-pass-through-in-v1-inventory-arrives-with-slicing.md) covers a known setup fee, while [ADR-0007](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/docs/adr/0007-margin-is-a-percentage-of-machine-time.md) chooses one multiplier on machine time.

- **The funding break remains even with accurate prices.** [PRV-13b](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/02-provider-contract.md#L173) reserves requested *time* and wind-down cost; [LDG-33](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/12-billing-and-ledger.md#L325) derives a runway date by dividing usable satoshis by a time rate. A burst of bytes has no corresponding runway duration. [LDG-31](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/12-billing-and-ledger.md#L254) clamps a debit to remaining authority and assigns the excess to the operator, including during quarantine or a rate outage; [ADR-0029](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/docs/adr/0029-during-a-price-outage-a-funding-cancellation-waits-for-the-rate-or-the-bound.md) deliberately delays funding cancellation without a rate. Provider termination can erase the last readable counter; [LDG-74](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/12-billing-and-ledger.md#L479) stops time metering on recorded absence, while [LDG-75](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/12-billing-and-ledger.md#L1888) can recognize an eventual invoice as operator payable, not retroactively grant tenant spending authority.

- **Smaller alternatives.** A traffic alarm is useful operationally, but Hetzner says it is not a cap. Powering off stops the workload yet [Cloud billing continues](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/billing/faq/); [LDG-13](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/12-billing-and-ledger.md#L1717) therefore uses cancellation for funding failure. Hetzner [Cloud firewalls](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/firewalls/faq/) have no byte quota and rule changes leave established connections active, so “stop egress at allowance” needs a proved cutoff and lag bound. [SEC-42](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/07-security-requirements.md#L187) can put conduct in terms, not cap a provider invoice. The strongest case against building a meter now is that an EU `cpx11` needs **days of sustained 300–500 Mbit/s outbound** to cross the reported allowance; actual workload incidence is unknown. That supports measuring demand before building (a), but does not make an alarm a funding bound.

No Lean declaration needs weakening for that gate. If (a) is chosen instead, its proof surface includes `Meter.postedDebit` and a new byte recurrence, `Funding.post`/`exitSubject`, `Runway.runwaySeconds`, `Ledger.clamp`/`nativeSplit`, `Period.current`, and `Payables.required` in [tools/formal/Provisiond](https://github.com/douglaz/provisiond/blob/ff4b4cc27fa729c96d1e641aaa9bba873fde5214/tools/formal/Provisiond).
