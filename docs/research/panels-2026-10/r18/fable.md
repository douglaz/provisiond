**Verdict: A, declared — the operator absorbs the remainder; declare the unit, record the loss, and build no charge until an invoice settles the provider's grid.**

**Why.** The story is true. Billable ends at the gone-write, not at dispatch or provider billing stop: `LDG-74` says "Billing stops at the observation instant" (`12-billing-and-ledger.md:510`), the exit closes the increment in that write (`:873`), and `LDG-32` releases the rest "in full" (`:297`). Nothing compensates but margin on consumed seconds: `wind_down_cost` is a reservation, no deficiency cause covers rounding (`:1931-1938`), and `LDG-37`'s provider-wins holds only "where one exists" (`:461`) — Hetzner's is an invoice arriving after release, when `LDG-31` says the excess "MUST NOT be taken from available balance" (`:275`). But B rests on two facts nobody has: where the hour is anchored and how it meets the monthly cap. Neither Hetzner page says (fetched today through a summarising tool). And the loss is small where the product is. With unit U and margin m, the operator loses on machines shorter than U/(1+m) (54.5 min at 10%, 50 at 20%), can lose up to U/m (10 h, 5 h), and breaks even on average only at a mean lifetime of U/2m (5 h, 2.5 h) — so `ADR-0007`'s own two-hour box loses on expectation. Worst case is one unit at cost per machine: €1.60 a day per order endpoint on Robot (20 orders at the note's €0.08, `provider-verify-2026-10.md:670`); on Cloud, `SEC-39`'s create ceiling times one hour at cost, per principal-hour. The ceiling is an integer with no default (`07-security-requirements.md:209-212`), tenants are self-serve, and a one-minute machine pays about 2% of what it costs, so the real bound is `order_budget`, which the deployment "MAY lower" (`02-provider-contract.md:884`).

**Defects in the framing**

1. **"The provider's price *is* one hour."** It is the lesser of the cap and rounded hours. The note's table says "monthly cap" (`provider-verify-2026-10.md:559`); the FAQ says "we will bill you for the minimum amount, whether that is the monthly price cap OR the hourly price multiplied by the number of hours you used the server" (re-check verbatim). The meter has no cap (`12-billing-and-ledger.md:635`; the cap exists only as a reserve bound, `02-provider-contract.md:374`). B copies the half of the provider's price that pays the operator, and bills a remainder, with margin, on a capped machine the provider charged nothing extra for.
2. **Grid.** Batch A's single server (`provider-verify-2026-10.md:651-668`) cannot separate creation-anchored from clock-anchored. Run one server from :50 to :10 (one billed hour means duration-based) and one across a month end.
3. **"Target market."** `ADR-0007` says "bursty and unattended" inside a rejected alternative, with a two-hour example (`docs/adr/0007-…:28-31`). `ADR-0010` says "**Hetzner Robot** is the product" (`docs/adr/0010-…:32`), limited to "20 requests per day" (`02-provider-contract.md:875`). The loop cannot multiply there.
4. **`billing_stop_window`.** Your reading beats the note's, but both assume an observable. `STO-53` records "when the engine first observed its effect" (`05-persistence.md:477`): visibility, which `PRV-36` says "MUST NOT be conflated" (`02-provider-contract.md:831`). The only billing-stop evidence is an invoice in whole units. Twenty samples are twenty remainders: expected maximum 0.95 U, 12% chance below 0.9 U.
5. **Reserve.** The note's "wind-down term therefore needs the rounding unit" (`provider-verify-2026-10.md:550`) is false under A. After the gone-write nothing debits, so a unit term freezes satoshis no debit can draw. Only B needs it.
6. **`ADR-0029`** is precedent for A, an accepted operator cost (`docs/adr/0029-…:109`).
7. **Omitted.** On 2026-10-03 `ADR-0011` rejected "a minimum charge of one meter interval" because it "overcharges partial use" (`docs/adr/0011-…:88`). B and a DigitalOcean-style minimum do too; only the unit's owner differs. A minimum still loses 53 minutes of cost on a 61-minute machine at 10%.
8. **B's mechanics.** The remainder is future time priced at exit, against "every increment is priced at a rate that held for the whole of it" (`12-billing-and-ledger.md:722`) and "future time charged at cancellation acceptance" (`:882-883`). A provider-terminated machine's unit is computed from the observation, not the provider's instant. `machine_attachments` has no attach instant (`:905`); an attached machine has "two defensible start instants" (`:89`). An outage exit has no rate to price it (`:1308`); a quarantined exit posts nothing (`:1016`). `stopped` and `cancellation_scheduled` remain billable, so neither is an exit.
9. **Lean.** B stops `Provisiond.Funding.lifecycle_coverage`, `unmarked_lifecycle_coverage` (charged seconds are exactly the seed-to-stop spans) and `release_follows_close` (the mark equals the stop boundary) from proving. `Provisiond.Meter` and `Provisiond.Period` survive. A contradicts neither an ADR nor a theorem.
10. **Confirmed.** `LDG-37`'s "partial period, rounded per `LDG-28`" is satoshi direction (`:477`, `:46`). `PRV-13c` names "billing granularity" (`02-provider-contract.md:396`), so it permits a declared unit, but `PRV-44` has no field for one (`:59-66`).
11. **A's own hole.** `LDG-75`'s accrual understates the payable by the remainder until invoiced — a sixth of the bill for ten-minute machines — and "Accepted accounting gaps" (`:1950`) does not list it. Its `true_up_minor` already reports the realised loss monthly; revisit B against that.

If the owner charges anyway, charge each started unit at its start, not a remainder at exit: `LDG-27` converts "once per obligation" when it is incurred (`:1664`). That needs no reserve term and no future rate, and still needs the grid.
